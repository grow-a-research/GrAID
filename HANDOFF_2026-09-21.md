## 2. What the essay results say

Full draft: **`CH4_ESSAY_RESULTS.md`** (repo root). Headline numbers:

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| Transcription accuracy (1 − CER) | 97.5% mean, 99.5% median, n = 54 |
| Pearson r, system vs. professor mean | 0.578 (p < .001) |
| Essay Score Agreement (within 10%) | 64.5% |
| Quadratic weighted kappa | 0.519 |
| Professor–professor pairwise kappa | 0.460 (range .361–.548) |
| **Leave-one-out: professor vs. other four** | **r = .598** |
| **Leave-one-out: system vs. same four** | **r = .565** |

The leave-one-out comparison is the main finding and the one to defend: measured
identically on both sides, the system agrees with a four-professor consensus about
as well as a professor does, and better than two of the five professors.

Do **not** lead with "kappa .519 beats the professors' .460." Those are not
comparable — a five-rater mean is less noisy than a single rater.

## 3. Sections removed from the results (deliberate)

Three analyses were run, produced real findings, and were then cut from Chapter 4 by
team decision. The numbers still exist in
`classification_model/essay_agreement_results.md` if anyone asks:

- **Score-range bias.** The system overscores the weakest quartile of essays by
  13.49% (only 30.0% within threshold) while performing at 73.6–80.0% on the upper
  three quartiles.
- **Confidence calibration.** The system's self-reported confidence does not predict
  its own error (r = .044). Per-criterion spread weakly does (r = .170).
- **Flag results.** 15 essay flags raised, 14 reviewed, all resolved "not an error."
  Flagged responses did have significantly worse transcription than unflagged ones
  (Mann-Whitney U = 384.5, p = .020).

If a panelist asks "what didn't work," the honest answer is the first one: weak
essays get overscored, which is exactly why the professor stays in the loop.

## 3b. Prompt-development overlap — raised, checked, resolved

A teammate audit found that essays used to choose the prompt wording also appear in
the evaluation set: 14 of 17 `fewshot_test` essays, 22 of 24 `anticompression_test`
essays, and — most directly — **all four few-shot exemplars**, whose professor
scores sit verbatim inside the prompt. A further 43 of the 200 had been scored by an
earlier prompt version during development.

All of it was verified against the data. The headline was then recomputed on the
essays prompt development never touched:

| Subset | n | r | QWK | Prof vs. four | System vs. four |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All essays (primary) | 200 | .578 | .519 | .598 | .565 |
| Minus prompt-dev + exemplars | 162 | .633 | .573 | .621 | .619 |
| Minus earlier-scored essays too | 127 | .636 | .565 | .609 | .622 |

**Agreement rises on the clean subsets.** On the 162, the system's agreement with a
four-professor consensus (.619) is effectively identical to a professor's own
(.621) — the gap falls from .034 to .002. The overlap was depressing the figures,
not inflating them, so the primary results are conservative.

This is written up as section 4.x.4 of `CH4_ESSAY_RESULTS.md` and is computed by
`run_essay_agreement.py` on every run. All-200 stays the primary result because that
sample was specified in advance; the subsets are a sensitivity check.

Three other audit findings needed no action: cross-variant file overlap (we make no
cross-variant comparison), the two conflicting band schemes (no band figure appears
anywhere now), and the broken coordinator regrade file (0 of its 15 essays are in
`final_200` — they were replaced). Note for anyone comparing: band-based QWK is a
different quantity from the score-based QWK reported in Chapter 4.

## 4. Reproducing the numbers

```bash
python -m classification_model.run_essay_agreement
```

Run from repo root with the venv active. Takes about a minute (2,000 bootstrap
resamples). Writes:

- `classification_model/essay_agreement_results.md` — aggregate stats, safe to share
- `classification_model/dress_data/essay_agreement_per_essay.csv` — per-essay, do NOT share

The script self-checks on every run: it rebuilds the professor consensus from the
raw per-professor scores and compares it against the stored consensus. Last run
matched to 0.000000 points. If that number is not ~0, stop and reconcile the files
before trusting anything downstream.

The QWK implementation was validated against `sklearn.metrics.cohen_kappa_score`
with quadratic weights, matching to floating-point precision.

## 8. Gotchas that will waste your time

**The live database keeps changing.** During this session essay responses with
reference transcriptions went 46 → 54, and essay answers went 88 → 74. Every
transcription and flag figure in Chapter 4 is a snapshot. **Freeze an export and
recompute before final submission.**

**The pickles are pandas-version-locked.** `_raw_professor_scores.pkl` was written
by pandas 3.x and will not load in the venv (pandas 2.3.3) — it raises
`NotImplementedError`. Use `_raw_professor_scores_combined.csv` instead; the script
already prefers it.

**The raw professor scores live in two files.** `_raw_professor_scores.pkl` holds
the *original* 200 essays including the 28 that were later replaced, so it covers
only 172 of the final dataset. The other 28 are in `_replacement_28_raw.pkl`. The
combined CSV merges both (228 rows). Anyone reconstructing this from scratch will
silently lose 28 essays if they use only the first file.

**One citation is unverified.** Mizumoto & Eguchi (2023) came via arXiv because the
publisher returned 403. Confirm from the original before citing. Emirtekin & Karasu
(2026) is abstract-only.

**Two survey respondents share a surname** ("Rogelio Q. Maniulit" and "ROGELIO Q.
MANIULIT JR."). Confirm whether n = 5 or 6 before reporting SUS figures.
